Our history

More than a lodge. Part of our town's story.

Chartered in 1843, St. John's Lodge No. 63 stands among the earliest Masonic lodges named on this site, and remains a lasting part of the heritage of Carleton Place.

A town on the Mississippi

The Town of Carleton Place records that the settlement was Morphy's Falls. In September 1819, settlement rights were issued to Edmond Morphy and his sons. The community was named Carleton Place in 1829.

The lodge is chartered

This site dates the lodge's charter to 1843. The lodge's own history page describes the written history as running from 1843 to 2018.

Brotherhood and charity

Through change and renewal, the lodge has served as a place of brotherhood, moral instruction, charity, and fellowship.

Our Journey Through Time

1843

Lodge Chartered

St. John's Lodge No. 63 is constituted under the Grand Lodge of Canada West, now the Grand Lodge of Ancient Free and Accepted Masons of Canada in the Province of Ontario.

1850s–1860s

Building Foundations

Membership grows and the Lodge establishes deep roots within Carleton Place. Early leaders guide the Lodge through its formative decades.

Early 1900s

Growth & Community

The Lodge continues to thrive alongside the development of Carleton Place, supporting charities and strengthening ties throughout the surrounding community.

1912

The Great Fire

A devastating fire sweeps through Carleton Place, destroying the Masonic Temple and much of the downtown core. The Lodge later relocates and continues its work from 55 Bridge Street.

Wartime Years

Service & Sacrifice

Members answer the call in times of war. The Lodge remains a steady source of Brotherhood, support, remembrance, and continuity throughout uncertain generations.

Modern Era

Preservation & Continuity

St. John's No. 63 continues its mission today, preserving tradition while welcoming new generations of good men into the Craft.
